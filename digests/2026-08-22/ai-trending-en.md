## 3. Trend Signal Analysis

The dominant signal is the shift from chat assistants to agent harnesses. The fastest-rising repos—[mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills), [superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers), [ECC](https://github.com/affaan-m/ECC), [ruflo](https://github.com/ruvnet/ruflo), and [apache/maka](https://github.com/apache/maka)—are not model apps; they are operating systems for coding agents: skill registries, memory, security, observability, and multi-agent orchestration. This indicates the community is focused on making agents reliable, auditable, and longer-lived, not just capable of one-off tasks.

A second signal is the rise of agent-native infrastructure: MCP-based observability ([PostHog](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog)), plugin specifications ([cursor/plugins](https://github.com/cursor/plugins)), and security gateways are becoming part of the standard stack. Third, local-first and self-hosted AI is now an explicit value proposition across agents, RAG, and model runtime. Related is the emergence of vectorless RAG—[PageIndex](https://github.com/VectifyAI/PageIndex) and [Graphify](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify)—promising cheaper, reasoning-based retrieval without vector stores. Fourth, efficiency tooling is resurging: [headroom](https://github.com/headroomlabs-ai/headroom) compresses tokens before they reach the LLM, and [tiny-llm](https://github.com/skyzh/tiny-llm) teaches inference internals—all responding to the cost pressure of agentic loops. Finally, open-weight model deployment remains tied to new releases: [ollama](https://github.com/ollama/ollama) now highlights Kimi-K2.6, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek, and Qwen, keeping local inference hot. The overall pattern: open source is building the control plane for AI agents, not just the models.
